# Broker approach and call script

Generic version of the Tumble Wash playbook ([broker-playbook.md](/mnt/project-files/broker/broker-playbook.md)). Fill the brackets for each deal. Background on how Ryan presents himself and on proof of funds is in [standing-decisions.md](standing-decisions.md).

**What wins a cash-only listing is certainty, not experience.** Brokers rank buyers by "will this close, on time, without drama?" Cash with no financing contingency, a flexible closing date, a real reason to be hands-on, and professionals lined up answer that question.

## Before the call
- **Text back the same day,** short and confident: "Hi [broker], thanks for the heads up. I'm a cash buyer, funds are ready, and I can move quickly. What time works best to talk?"
- **Visit as a customer first if you can.** Don't tell staff the store is for sale. Note machines running, out-of-order signs, cleanliness, wash-and-fold bags waiting, card readers, parking and foot traffic. Go back at a peak time (weekend morning) before signing anything.
- **Line up a business attorney and an accountant,** even informally, so you can name them.

## The call (20–30 minutes). Goal: be the buyer the broker brings offers from. Don't name a price.

**1. Open (about 60 seconds, then stop)**
> "Thanks for making time, [broker]. Quick background so you know who you're dealing with. I'm a commercial real estate broker, so I live in leases, landlords and closings, and I'm moving into owning and running an operating business myself. I'm the operating partner, and my family is my capital partner. It's an all-cash purchase with no financing contingency, and I can send proof of funds today. I plan to be in the store, especially the first year. I'd like to come see it [this week / next week]. Before we get into it, how are things going on your end?"

**2. Ask about the activity (curious, not anxious)**
> "What does the interest look like? Are people touring, or are offers already in?"
> "Is there a deadline or a best-and-final date?"
> "What has kept other buyers from moving so far?"

If offers are in: "What would make an offer stand out to the seller besides price?" Don't react to a number; say "Good to know, thanks."

**3. Learn what the seller wants**
> "What matters most to the seller: timing, the staff, a clean handoff?"
> "When does the seller need to be done, and how long could they stay on to train a new owner?"
> "Do the employees plan to stay?"

**4. Ask for the documents**
> "Coming from commercial real estate, the lease is the first thing I look at. Could you send the full lease and amendments, the landlord's name, and whether the landlord will consent to an assignment? I'm happy to sign your NDA today."
> "Could you also send 24 to 36 months of the card-system reports, monthly, plus the wash-and-fold POS reports? And the water, gas and electric bills for the same period, the tax returns, and an equipment list with ages and service history."

If asked why so much: "So I can get you a clean offer quickly and not come back later with surprises." The full list is in [checklist.md](checklist.md).

**5. If pushed on price**
> "I'd rather put a real number in writing once I've seen the lease and the reports, so it holds up. You'll get it quickly."
> "I'm a serious cash buyer and I'll be competitive. I just want the number based on the records so neither of us has to revisit it."

Raise the model's issues lightly, as questions: "The machines are about [age] years old. Has the seller priced in a replacement?" / "The P&L leaves out [payroll tax, workers' comp, electric]. Do you have the actual numbers?"

**6. Book the visit and close**
> "What days work for you and the seller next week? I'd love to meet them if they're open to it. I'll keep it quiet with the staff."
> "Before I make an offer, could I shadow the seller for two or three shifts, including a Saturday?"
> "I'll send the NDA and proof of funds right after this call. What's the best email?"

**Don't say:** "my parents are paying for it" as an opener, that it's passive, a price or that you'll pay full price, or anything you'd have to walk back.

## Messages

**Email after the call, same day** (subject: [Store] – [your name] – proof of funds and next steps)
> Hi [broker],
>
> Thanks for the time today. As discussed, I'm a cash buyer who will own and run the store, with no financing contingency and a flexible closing date.
>
> Attached: proof of funds [and my family's funding letter] and the signed NDA.
>
> To get my attorney started, could you send:
> - The lease and all amendments, plus the landlord's contact for assignment
> - 24 months of card-system and wash-and-fold POS reports
> - 12–24 months of water, gas and electric bills
> - The last 3 years of tax returns and the equipment list
>
> Once I've reviewed the lease and reports I'm ready to submit a written offer quickly. My attorney is [name, firm, email].
>
> Best,
> [Name] · [phone]

**If there are other offers:** "Understood. I'm ready to move. If there's a deadline for offers, let me know and I'll have mine in before it."

**When the offer goes in:** "[Broker], offer sent to your email: all cash, $[x] deposit, short diligence, closing on the seller's timing. Happy to jump on a call."

**After a visit:** "Stopped by the store this morning. Clean, busy, and the location makes sense. Looking forward to next steps."

## Giving the broker what they want
Brokers are paid by the seller at closing. Give them fast, complete responses, no retrades (every condition is in the LOI from day one), professionals in place, and respect for their expertise ("What do new owners usually get wrong in the first 90 days?"). "If this goes well I'd like to look at a second store with you" works only if you mean it.

## Path to closing (typical)
1. Text and visit as a customer. 2. Call; NDA. 3. Proof of funds. 4. Engage attorney and accountant. 5. Review lease and reports. 6. Written LOI with protections. 7. Meet the seller. 8. Diligence: reconcile reports to returns and deposits, utility bills, machine inspection, payroll, licenses, peak-hour visit. 9. Form OpCo and Ryan's S-corp LLC (3+ weeks before close). 10. Purchase agreement and deposit. 11. Landlord assignment and guaranty terms (often the slowest step). 12. Bulk-sale notice. 13. Insurance including workers' comp. 14. Registrations and payroll. 15. Transfers: card system, POS, ATM, utilities, vendors, alarm. 16. Walkthrough, wire, close. 17. Seller training; start the hours log.

Realistic timeline: LOI to close in about 6–10 weeks. Lease consent and the bulk-sale notice set the earliest date, so start both early.
